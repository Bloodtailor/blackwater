# BLACKWATER

### Round-based zombies, forty fathoms down.

In 1968, a naval site at the bottom of a flooded sinkhole drilled straight down toward something warm — and drowned mid-shift, all forty-one men. No bodies were ever recovered. The file was sealed. The paperwork simply stops.

You are **Vernon Lowe** — the diver families hire when the official search is called off. A client you will never meet is paying you $1.4 million to bring one item up from the bottom of the bore. The item is warm to the touch. The enclosed photograph of its chamber was developed three years *after* the site flooded. You asked. No one answered. The fee cleared anyway.

**BLACKWATER is round-based survival the way you remember it mattering** — points, wall buys, a mystery box that moves, perks, Pack-a-Punch — rebuilt in the one place the genre has never been: underwater, in the dark, inside a cave that does not want you to find your way back. And the deadliest thing down there isn't the dead. It's your air gauge.

**⬛ Air is the only clock that matters.** There is no camping, no crawler to babysit, no safe corner to farm from. Every errand — the box, the power switch, the perk you're saving for — is a dive, and every dive is paid for in breath. When a round ends, the cave gives you forty seconds. Then the shift bell rings whether you're ready or not.

**⬛ You cannot win by outlasting.** Rounds scale forever *on purpose*. Build too slow and the dark outgrows you; dive too greedy and you die rich at the bottom of a hole. Get powerful, get deep, take the item, and get out — every point you spend is a move in a race you can actually win. Or actually lose.

**⬛ The cave must be learned, because it will not help you.** Lay your own guide line and follow it home by feel when the silt takes your eyes. Drop chemlights to mark the dead ends. Memorize the junctions before the currents roll your sense of *up* into the wall. Your bubbles always rise toward the surface. Some nights, they're the only thing down there that tells the truth.

**⬛ Fear that plays fair.** Brush the wrong pale formation and the chamber whites out in silt for a long, crawling minute — and something in the silt moves faster than you do. A warm lamp glows in a side passage where no lamp should be lit. Two condemned diving suits stand watch at the drill head, and the equipment log lists both as *returned empty*. Every horror has a counter. Every counter must be earned.

**⬛ The site is still working, and nobody will tell you why.** The lockers are greased and stocked. The reactor holds a banked ember. The draughts are still potable after forty years, the jukebox is still wired, and somewhere in the dark three wind-up toys are waiting to be found by somebody who still counts as *somebody*.

**⬛ The count is wrong.** The roster says forty-one men. You will put down hundreds, and the site will remain fully staffed. The same faces keep coming back — you'll start to recognize them; Lowe already does. The stencil at the winch head reads `COMPLEMENT: 41 — FULL`, and in forty years it has never needed repainting. Keep your tally anyway. Lowe does. It's the last honest arithmetic in the water — right up until it isn't.

One diver. One breath at a time. One item, carried — not rigged, not bagged — to open daylight.

> *Site lines are condemned — lay your own.*
> *Do not photograph the item.*
> *Do not correct the count.*

**The site is at full complement. You are the discrepancy.**
